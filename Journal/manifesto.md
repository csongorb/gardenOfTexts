# Research / Artist Statement / Manifesto (?)

## 2026-10-04: OG

I'm trying to remember my original motivation(s).  
On the one hand: this was when I made the *garden - game design* connection / realisation. When we had the *Game Design as Gardening* summit. When we came up with the *how to grow a game* subtitle for Devolution. When I had my first classes about about all this (*GD as Gardening* and *Game Development Simulator*).  
But also: I mean obviously: my garden. My (deeper?) fascination with its beauty. Seeing it grow. Seeing how my actions influence its future. Bending / shaping it (is it power-play?).  
And of course: It wasn't meant to be a... game? But was happy to send it as an interactive birthday-card to a game designer friend. I have made some similar flower simulation experiments before. But this came together in a very short amount of time, the writing gave the idea momentum.

I want this to be:  
Fun to *observe*, especially how the writing comes together, but also falls apart. How it morphs and has moments that are beatiful and/or nice to see. Pattern watching.  
Fun to *indirectly control*. Culture vs. nature. The tension.  
And fun to *give* it to someone else. The question is: is it only fun for me as the one who have made it? But how could it also be fun for everyone?

Yes, that was the time where I have read Pollan, it still makes a lot of sense, for this game, for gardening, for game design, generally:

> The green thumb is the gardener who can nimbly walk the line between the dangers of over- and undercultivation, between pushing nature too far and giving her too much ground. [...] To occupy such a middle ground is not easy - the temptation is always to either take complete control or relinquish it altogether, to invoke your own considerable (but in the end overrated) power or to bend to nature's. (Michael Pollan: Second Nature)

And I like that it mostly goes with my stupid (and also not really true / possible) comment that I'm only killing the plants in my garden cause they grow too fast.
